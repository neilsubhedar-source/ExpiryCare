Healthcare Guidance

General awareness on storing and handling expiry-sensitive products.

Important note

Please note: This information is for general awareness only and is not a substitute for professional medical advice. Always consult a qualified healthcare professional for medical concerns.

Medicine Storage

Most medicines should be kept in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight and humidity, unless the label specifies refrigeration. Keep medicines in their original packaging so the expiry date and batch information remain visible.

Food / Product Safety

Check both the expiry date and the condition of packaging before use. Discard groceries that show signs of spoilage even if the expiry date has not yet passed.

Personal Care Product Safety

Personal-care products such as sunscreen, cosmetics and skincare items can lose effectiveness or become unsafe after their expiry date. Note the “period after opening” symbol on packaging where present.

Expiry Awareness

Regularly reviewing your inventory helps you catch products approaching expiry before they become a health risk or go to waste. Use the Expiry Alerts page to stay on top of items that need attention.

Reading dates and batch codes

Look for EXP, Use by, Best before, MFG or Manufactured on. Batch/lot codes identify a production run; do not assume a code can be decoded without the manufacturer's method.

When a date is missing

Keep the item marked Expiry Unknown and set a review reminder. Check the full package, contact the manufacturer, or ask a pharmacist about medicines. A reminder is not an expiry date.

Storage and disposal

Follow the product label and local disposal guidance. Keep medicines in original packaging. Do not pour unknown chemicals down drains or mix them; seek local hazardous-waste advice.

Common misconception

A barcode usually identifies a product and does not necessarily contain its expiry date. Product recognition or a readable label is not proof of safety.
